# Roland MC-303 Groovebox — Sound Banks & Sample Matrix

Released in 1996, the **Roland MC-303** was the first instrument to carry the "Groovebox" name. Built on a JV-80-derived sample-based (rompler) sound engine with 28-voice polyphony and an 8-part multitimbral sequencer, it packs **448 preset tones** (addressed via MIDI Program Change `1–127` and Bank Select MSB `CC0 = 64–74`, `CC32 = 0`) and **12 Rhythm Sets** (addressed on MIDI Channel 10 / Bank `121`).

> **Note on Famous Usage:** Unlike the analog **Roland TB-303** (which defined acid house on its own), the MC-303 was aimed at late-1990s home producers and DJs, and its preset patterns were mostly trance and techno. The only MC-303 users I could document (via Equipboard) are **Vladislav Delay** (his first piece of equipment), **:wumpscut:** (lists the MC-303 among its gear in a 1997 interview) and **Redman** (Equipboard cites MTV Cribs). **No famous track is documented against any individual MC-303 bank**: the artist names in the "Direct MC-303 Usage" lines and the matrix below (including Cex, Groove Armada and Basement Jaxx) are illustrative and unverified. Named tracks refer to the **classic Roland/analog hardware** (TB-303, TR-808, TR-909, Juno, Jupiter-8, SH-101, CR-78, TR-606) whose sounds the MC-303 samples, not to the MC-303 itself.

## How to Select a Sound on the MC-303 Front Panel

The MC-303 organizes its 448 tones into **29 banks** by instrument type. On the display, the **left side** is the **bank** (`T1`–`T29`) and the **right side** is the **tone number** within that bank (`01`–`99`). Every tone table below has a **Panel (Bank · No.)** column — for example `T2 · 05` means bank `T2` (Synth Bass), tone `05` (`acid TB 2`).

1. Press a **PART** button for Parts 1–7 (melodic) — or the **R** part for drum kits.
2. Use **SELECT** `[◄][►]` until the **TONE** indicator lights.
3. Turn the **VALUE** dial to pick the tone. Hold **[SHIFT]** while turning to jump the tens digit quickly.

- **Parts 1–7 (tones):** left display `T1`–`T29`, right display `01`–`99`. Look in the **Panel** column of banks 01–29 below.
- **Part R (rhythm sets):** left display `d01`–`d12`, right display `909` / `808` / `JAZ` … Look in the **Rhythm Sets** list at the bottom.

> Banks `T20`–`T29` are the single-hit percussion/drum tones; "Kit" tones spread several drum sounds across the keys.

> **Reading on a phone:** the summary matrix and the per-bank tone tables below are wide data tables, so they scroll sideways. The rest of this file fits the screen.

## MIDI Bank Select Cross-Reference (CC0# 64–74 & Bank 121)

In MIDI/DAW patch maps (such as `.reabank` files), the 448 tones are addressed using **Bank Select MSB (`CC0 = 64–74`, `CC32 = 0`)** paired with **Program Change (`1–127` in 1-based manuals, `0–126` in 0-based DAWs)**:

- **Bank 64 (`CC0 = 64`)**: Primary/base variation for all tone categories (119 patches).
- **Banks 65–74 (`CC0 = 65–74`)**: Progressive variations per program number (e.g., `lead TB 1` at `1-64`, `lead TB 2` at `1-65`, `wow TB` at `1-66`, `lead TB 3` at `1-67`).
- **Bank 121 (`CC0 = 121`)**: The 12 dedicated **Rhythm Sets** (drum kits) on MIDI Channel 10.
